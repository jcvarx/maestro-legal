Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-08-28

Maestro is a local, approval-first tool used to prepare and manage social media campaigns for an independent musician. This page explains, in plain language, how Maestro handles data. It is provided for an early-stage, private beta and is not a guarantee of a commercial service.

Local-first by design

Maestro runs on the user's own computer. Release information, draft campaigns, generated content, and settings are stored locally on that computer unless the user chooses to connect a third-party platform or AI provider (see below). Maestro does not operate a central server that collects user data.

What stays local, and what leaves your computer

Stays local, always: your release files (audio, artwork, video, lyrics), draft campaigns, generated captions and content, app settings, and any connected-platform credentials or access tokens. None of this is sent to Maestro's developers or to any server Maestro operates — because Maestro doesn't operate one.

Sent to a connected platform (TikTok, YouTube, or Instagram) — only when you've explicitly connected that platform and explicitly approved a specific publish: the approved content itself (e.g. the video, image, caption, or title), sent directly from your computer to that platform's own API. Maestro does not relay this through any server of its own.

Sent to an AI provider — only when you've configured one. By default Maestro generates captions and content entirely offline, on your own computer, with no external AI call at all. If you choose to configure an external AI provider instead, the specific release/campaign content needed to generate a response (for example, the song title, genre, or lyrics you've supplied) is sent to that provider's API to produce the draft. That provider's own privacy policy governs how they handle it; Maestro does not send anything to an AI provider unless you've explicitly turned this on.

TikTok specifically

Maestro requests the video.publish permission from TikTok, used only to post content you've explicitly approved to your own connected TikTok account. TikTok's own login process separately includes basic profile identification as a standard part of connecting any account (via TikTok Login Kit) — Maestro uses this only to maintain the connection itself and does not read, store, or share it beyond that. Maestro does not access your TikTok followers, direct messages, or any content beyond what you explicitly approve for posting.

Credentials and access tokens

Access tokens and credentials for connected platforms are stored locally in the user's own environment. They are not transmitted to Maestro's developers or any third party other than the platform they belong to. Keeping these values secure is the user's responsibility — they should never be shared or committed to a public location.

No automatic publishing

Maestro does not post, publish, or send anything on a connected platform automatically. Every publishing action requires the user's explicit, human approval before it happens.

No sale of personal data

Maestro does not sell personal data.

Cookies and analytics

This website does not use cookies, tracking, or analytics. Maestro itself is local software, not a hosted web service, and does not track usage.

Revoking access, and deleting your data

You can revoke Maestro's access to any connected platform at any time, directly from that platform's own account settings (for example, TikTok's or Google's connected-apps page). Doing so immediately invalidates the corresponding access token.

Because Maestro has no server-side account, there is nothing to delete on our end. Deleting the Maestro application and its local data folder from your computer removes everything it stored — releases, drafts, credentials, and settings alike.

What we don't claim

This is an early-stage private beta, not a certified or audited service. We don't claim compliance with any specific data-protection certification or framework, don't guarantee encryption of locally stored files beyond whatever your own operating system already provides, and don't commit to a specific data-retention period — your data stays on your own device for as long as you keep it there, and is removed when you remove it.

Changes to this policy

This policy may be updated as Maestro develops. The "Last updated" date above reflects the most recent change.
